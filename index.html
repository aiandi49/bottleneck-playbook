BEFORE YOU BUILD ANYTHING

Why, when, where, and how to start

These aren't from any single case study — they're the questions worth answering honestly before you commit real time or money.

Why

Start for a reason, not a feeling

A business only makes sense as a solution to a specific problem — yours or someone else's. Wanting independence is a motivation, not a business.

Do: Pick a problem you already understand deeply, from real experience or personal pain.
Don't: Start a business mainly to escape a job you dislike — that reason evaporates under pressure.

Stuck on the fear itself? Vague fears feel infinite — specific ones don't. "I might fail" has no answer. "What if I ask 100 people and all 100 say no" does: you adjust the offer and ask 100 more.

When

Start when you can afford to be wrong

The right time isn't when everything feels safe. It's when you have enough of a cushion to survive testing an idea that might not work the first time.

Do: Build a runway of personal savings, or keep income flowing while you validate the idea on the side.
Don't: Quit stable income the same week inspiration hits. Test it nights and weekends first.
Where

Start where you already have an edge

"Where" means both market and format — online, in person, product, or service. The right choice is usually the one where you already have some advantage.

Do: Start in a market where you already have credibility, access, or a relevant skill — even a small one.
Don't: Chase whatever industry is trending if you have zero real connection to it.
How

Start by selling, not by building

The sequence matters more than the polish. Get the simplest possible version in front of a real, paying customer before you invest in building anything further.

Do: Get one paying customer before you build the version you actually imagine.
Don't: Spend months building in private. Nothing survives contact with a real customer unchanged.

The concrete version: an entity, a bank account, a way to accept payment, and asking one stranger for money in exchange for something. Do those four things and the title has already changed from "aspiring" to "owner."

About Compass

Compass is an independent, educational library created to make real business turnarounds — and the thinking behind them — easier to actually apply. The case-study playbooks are drawn from coaching conversations filmed by business educator Alex Hormozi and published publicly on YouTube as part of "The Game w/ Alex Hormozi" and its "Scale or Fail" series. Founder Field Notes is drawn from a separate long-form conversation between Alex Hormozi and Steven Bartlett on "The Diary Of A CEO" podcast.

This hub is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced by Alex Hormozi, Acquisition.com, Steven Bartlett, or "The Diary Of A CEO." All figures and ideas are approximate and paraphrased from those publicly available conversations for teaching purposes. For the original conversations and additional resources, see the source videos.

Educational content only. Every business is different — treat these as starting sequences to test against your own numbers, not a guarantee of results.
